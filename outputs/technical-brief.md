# Technical Brief

**Purpose:** a short, focused document on a single technology, method, product feature, or
operational issue — problem to solution to benefit, quickly.
**Audience:** technical readers and informed decision-makers.
**Length:** 800–2,000 words.
**Pairs with:** `engineering-technical-analysis`, `quick-research-briefing`,
`standards-regulatory-research`.

## Required structure

### I. Cover page or title block
Full cover for shared documents; a simple title block (title, date, author, document type) is
fine for internal circulation.

### II. Introduction (2–3 paragraphs)
State the challenge or topic directly. One paragraph of context maximum — this format moves fast.

### III. Technical discussion (core of the document)
The solution, method, or technology with supporting detail: how it works, what it depends on,
where its limits are. Claims about performance, compatibility, or maturity carry citations and
confidence labels (**High / Moderate / Low / Inference**).

### IV. Benefits and results
Quantify wherever possible; speak in operational outcomes (throughput, cost, uptime, energy,
cycle time) rather than feature language. Estimates labeled as estimates, with method.

### V. Conclusion
Brief summary and a concrete next step or decision point.

### VI. About `[COMPANY]` (optional)
`[BOILERPLATE]` short version. Omit if writing unbranded.

### VII. Sources
Compact list: title, publisher, date, URL. Internal sources marked **[INTERNAL]**.

## Writing standards

- One subject per brief; if a second theme is growing, it's a second brief.
- Tables for specifications and comparisons; prose for reasoning.
- No promotional superlatives; measured, specific language throughout.
- Descriptive headings; the brief should be scannable in 60 seconds.
