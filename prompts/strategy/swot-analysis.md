# SWOT Analysis

Produce an evidence-backed SWOT — every entry cited or explicitly flagged as inference; an
uncited SWOT entry is an opinion wearing a framework:

**Subject:** [company, product, technology, or market position]

Required for a useful answer:
**Perspective:** [whose SWOT this is — the subject's own planning? a competitor evaluating them? an acquirer?]

Optional — fill in or delete:
**Decision this informs:** [bid, invest, partner, defend, enter]
**Internal sources available:** [our data about the subject or ourselves — marked INTERNAL]

## Before you begin

- **If you have web search or browsing, using it is required** — filings, product literature,
  reviews, press, hiring patterns, certifications. Strengths and weaknesses are claims about the
  present; the present is searchable.
- **If you have internal data access** and the subject is us (or a competitor we track), use
  win/loss records, field reports, and performance data, marked **[INTERNAL]**.
- **If you have no search tools**, say so and label the entire SWOT as unverified knowledge with
  its cutoff date.
- If subject or perspective is ambiguous, ask up to 2 clarifying questions first — the same
  facts sort into different quadrants depending on whose SWOT it is.

## Analysis protocol

1. **Research pass** — Gather the evidence before sorting anything: current offering and
   performance, market position, published financials or scale signals, customer sentiment,
   recent moves, and the external environment (market trends, regulation, technology shifts,
   competitive pressure).
2. **Classification** — Sort findings into the four quadrants with discipline:
   - **Strengths / Weaknesses** — internal to the subject and true today.
   - **Opportunities / Threats** — external and forward-looking.
   - A "strength" that describes the market is an opportunity, and vice versa. Present-day facts
     go in S/W; futures go in O/T.
3. **Entry rules** — Per quadrant, maximum 5–6 entries, each: one specific line (numbers beat
   adjectives), a citation or **[INTERNAL]** flag, and a confidence label (**H / M / L / Inf**).
   Twelve strengths is a brainstorm, not an analysis. If a quadrant's evidence is thin, say so
   in the quadrant instead of padding it.
4. **Synthesis (the so-what)** — Which strength meets which opportunity (where to push); which
   weakness meets which threat (what could hurt most); and the one move the pattern most
   supports, from the stated perspective.
5. **Watch items** — 3–5 developments that would move an entry between quadrants or change its
   weight, and where to watch for them.

## Evidence standards

- Specific beats general: "certified to [standard] with documented 15% cost advantage (source,
  year)" beats "strong manufacturing."
- Marketing claims attributed as claims; competitor intentions labeled **Inference**.
- Cross-check the entries the synthesis depends on; flag `single source`.
- Never fabricate a figure, quote, or source.

## Deliverable

Follow `outputs/swot-one-pager.md` if attached (2×2 with header, so-what line, watch items).
Otherwise: header (subject, perspective, as-of date, capability disclosure) → four quadrants
with cited entries → synthesis → watch items → compact source list (internal separate).

Before you deliver, check: perspective stated; internal/external discipline enforced; every
entry cited or flagged; quadrants capped; synthesis names specific pairings, not platitudes.
