# Market Research Report

**Purpose:** a decision-grade picture of a market — size, structure, demand, competition, and
direction — with every number traceable to a source and method.
**Audience:** strategy, product, and business development; readers who will build plans on these
numbers.
**Length:** 5–20 pages.
**Pairs with:** `market-research-analysis`, `competitive-landscape-analysis`, `swot-analysis`.

## Required structure

### 1. Title block
Market, geography, time horizon, as-of date, capability disclosure (web search / internal data /
knowledge only).

### 2. Executive summary (written last)
Market size and growth headline, the two or three structural facts that matter most, the biggest
risk to the thesis, and overall confidence.

### 3. Market definition and segmentation
What is and is not in scope (products, applications, geographies, customer types) — stated
before any numbers, because every disagreement between published market sizes starts with a
definition mismatch. Segmentation with the logic behind it.

### 4. Market size and growth
- **TAM / SAM / SOM** (or the subset that makes sense), each with: the figure, the year, the
  source, and the **method** (top-down from published totals vs. bottom-up from units × price).
- Published estimates that disagree are presented as a range with the definitional reason for
  the spread — never silently averaged.
- Growth rates with drivers, not just CAGRs.
- Every figure here is either cited or labeled **estimate** with its calculation shown.

### 5. Demand analysis
Who buys, why, how the purchase decision works (buying criteria, cycle length, budget owners),
and what is changing in demand. Voice-of-customer evidence where available; marked **[INTERNAL]**
if it came from company CRM/sales data.

### 6. Competitive landscape (summary)
Major players, shares where published, positioning, and recent moves. If a full
`competitive-landscape-analysis` was run, summarize and reference it rather than duplicating.

### 7. Trends, drivers, and barriers
Technology, regulatory, and economic forces — each with direction, evidence, and a timeframe.
Separate established trends from emerging signals from speculation.

### 8. Outlook and scenarios
Base / upside / downside, each with the assumptions that produce it and the leading indicators
that would signal which scenario is unfolding.

### 9. Limitations and confidence
Mandatory. Where data was thin, paywalled, stale, or proprietary; which numbers are soft; what
primary research (interviews, surveys, paid reports) would harden them.

### 10. Source appendix
Every source: title, publisher, date, URL, access date, what it supports. Paid/analyst reports
identified as such. **[INTERNAL]** sources in a separate table.

## Writing standards

- Numbers carry sources and years inline; a market-size claim without both is a defect.
- Confidence labels (**High / Moderate / Low / Inference**) on all load-bearing findings.
- Charts and tables over prose for quantitative comparisons; prose for causal reasoning.
- No vendor-report language ("booming", "explosive growth") — direction, magnitude, and evidence.
