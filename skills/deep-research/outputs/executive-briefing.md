# Executive Briefing

**Purpose:** the answer without the journey — a decision-ready distillation of a research run.
**Audience:** executives, sponsors, anyone deciding rather than investigating.
**Length:** 1–2 pages, hard limit. If it doesn't fit, it isn't distilled yet.
**Pairs with:** any prompt; commonly generated alongside a full `research-report.md` as its
front door.

## Required structure

### 1. Bottom line up front (2–4 sentences)
The question asked and the answer found, including the overall confidence in that answer. No
throat-clearing, no background.

### 2. Key points (3–5)
One short paragraph each: the point, the strongest piece of evidence for it (with citation), and
its confidence label (**High / Moderate / Low / Inference**). Lead with the point, not the
evidence.

### 3. Opportunities and risks
Two short lists. Every entry carries a timeframe (now / 6–18 months / 2+ years) and, where the
evidence supports one, a likelihood.

### 4. Recommended actions
Numbered, specific, and owned ("who should do what by when"), even if the owner is provisional.
If the research doesn't support recommendations, say so and state what would.

### 5. Watch list
The 3–6 metrics, events, or decisions that would change this briefing's conclusions, and the
direction of change to watch for.

### 6. Basis
One line: capability disclosure (web search / internal data / knowledge only), as-of date, and a
pointer to the full report if one exists. Sources appear as a compact list — one line each, not
an appendix.

## Writing standards

- Standalone: the reader should need nothing else to make the decision at hand.
- Every claim traceable — compact citations, but real ones. No fabricated sources.
- Internal-data findings marked **[INTERNAL]**.
- No promotional language, no hedging padding; uncertainty is expressed through the confidence
  labels, not adverbs.
