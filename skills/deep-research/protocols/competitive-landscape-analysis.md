# Competitive Landscape Analysis

Analyze the competitive landscape, from **public sources only**, for:

**Domain:** [market/product category, and our offering if relevant]

Optional — fill in or delete:
**Our position:** [who we are in this market — shapes what "competitor" means]
**Competitors to include:** [named competitors, if known; otherwise discover them]
**Decision this informs:** [positioning, pricing, bid, roadmap, acquisition]
**Internal sources available:** [win/loss records, field intel — marked INTERNAL]

## Before you begin

- **If you have web search or browsing, using it is required** — competitor websites, product
  literature, press releases, financial filings, job postings, patents, reviews, and trade press
  are the raw material of this analysis.
- **If you have internal data access**, add win/loss records and field intelligence, marked
  **[INTERNAL]** and kept separate — internal perception and public reality often disagree, and
  the disagreement is itself a finding.
- **Public sources only.** No pretexting, no scraping behind logins, nothing that wouldn't
  survive being read aloud in court. If information isn't public, record it as a blind spot.
- If the market boundary is unclear, ask up to 2 clarifying questions; otherwise state the
  boundary you're using.

## Research protocol

1. **Competitor identification** — Direct competitors, indirect/substitute offerings, and
   emerging entrants, with one line each on why they qualify. State the discovery method so
   omissions can be judged.
2. **Competitor profiles** — For each significant player: offering and claimed differentiation;
   target segments; pricing where published; scale indicators (revenue, headcount, installed
   base — as published); recent moves (launches, wins, hires, filings — dated); and observable
   strengths and weaknesses, each tied to evidence, not reputation.
3. **Positioning map** — Choose the two or three dimensions that actually differentiate in this
   market (say why), and place the players. Note where positions are converging or vacant.
4. **Dynamics** *(optional — include when the decision is strategic rather than tactical)* —
   Five-forces-style read: barriers to entry, supplier/buyer power, substitute pressure, and
   rivalry intensity, each grounded in the evidence gathered above.
5. **Whitespace and threats** — Underserved segments or unmet needs the evidence supports; which
   competitor is best positioned to hurt us and how.
6. **Monitoring list** — The events worth watching per key competitor (product launches, key
   hires, certifications, filings) and where to watch for them.

## Evidence standards

- Every profile claim cites its source (title, publisher, date, URL); marketing claims are
  attributed as claims, not adopted as facts.
- Scale and share figures carry year and source; conflicting figures shown as ranges.
- Distinguish observed behavior (shipped products, signed customers) from announced intentions.
- Confidence labels: **High / Moderate / Low / Inference** — competitor strategy is usually
  Inference, and must say so.
- Blind spots named explicitly: private-company financials, unpublished pricing, undisclosed
  roadmaps.
- Never fabricate a competitor claim, figure, or source.

## Deliverable

Follow `outputs/research-report.md` if attached; profiles in comparable table format where
possible. Otherwise: executive summary → identification → profiles → positioning map →
(dynamics) → whitespace and threats → monitoring list → limitations → source appendix.

Before you deliver, check: discovery method stated; every profile evidence-based with dated
recent moves; positioning dimensions justified; internal vs public findings separated; blind
spots honest.
