# Quick Research Briefing

Give me a rapid, evidence-grounded briefing on:

**Topic:** [topic]

Optional — fill in or delete:
**Audience/occasion:** [who I'm talking to, what meeting this is for]
**Internal sources available:** [internal data sources, if any]

## Before you begin

- **If you have web search or browsing, using it is required.** This briefing is about what is
  true *now*; do not answer from memory alone.
- **If you have access to internal company data** (drives, wikis, document stores), search it for
  this topic too, and mark anything from it **[INTERNAL]**.
- **If you have no search tools**, say so up front, state your knowledge cutoff, and label the
  whole briefing as unverified training knowledge.
- If the topic is ambiguous, ask me up to 2 clarifying questions before starting; otherwise state
  your working interpretation in one line and go.

## Briefing framework

1. **Current reality check** — What is actually happening right now? Lead with the most recent
   verified development and its date.
2. **Core architecture** — The 3–4 essential components, mechanisms, or concepts I need to
   understand this topic at all.
3. **Live tensions** — The meaningful disagreements, trade-offs, or unresolved challenges. Not
   "some people think X, others think Y" — what is at stake, for whom, and what evidence each
   side has.
4. **Reality anchors** — Specific recent examples, concrete data points, or measurable outcomes
   that make this tangible. Prefer things that happened in the last 1–2 years, with dates.
5. **Sharp questions** — 3–4 questions that would reveal whether someone actually understands
   this topic: questions that expose assumptions, reveal trade-offs, or probe mechanisms.
6. **Go deeper** — 3–5 sources actually worth my time, **each one a source you found and can link,
   not one you recall**: title, publisher, date, URL, and one line on why it earns its place.

## Evidence standards

- Cite the claims that matter (publisher, date, URL) — compactly, this is a briefing not a paper.
- Flag anything that is dated, uncertain, conflicting, or single-source.
- Separate hype and speculation from substantiated trends.
- Never invent a source, statistic, or quote. "I could not verify this" is a valid finding.
- Label key claims with confidence: **High** (multiple independent sources), **Moderate**
  (consistent but limited), **Low** (weakly corroborated), or **Inference** (reasoned, not
  sourced — and say so in the sentence).

## Delivery

Conversational but sharp — you are briefing someone who needs to ask intelligent questions, not
recite facts. 600–1,200 words. State the as-of date at the top. If an output template file is
attached, follow it exactly instead.

Before you deliver, check: every section present; most recent development dated; at least one
number with a source; no unverified reading-list entries; confidence labels on the load-bearing
claims.
